# Seattle Day Planner — visual direction

## World

**A pocket field guide with an annotated city line.** The experience treats a Seattle day like a page pulled from a well-loved travel notebook: supplied photography provides the sense of place, a rose route line draws the day across a schematic city map, and short editorial notes keep the interface human rather than logistical.

## Surface mode

Operate. Visitors are browsing places, choosing stops, and shaping an itinerary. The page keeps discovery expressive while making the selected route, ordering controls, and map legible at a glance.

## First viewport

The opening view pairs a large editorial statement with a Space Needle photograph. The planner follows immediately: selected stops on the left, the route's shape on the right. A visitor can understand the purpose and make a first choice without scrolling through a generic card grid.

## Materials and type

- Warm off-white paper-like ground and clean white surfaces.
- Deep rose as the single interaction and route accent.
- Supplied Seattle photography with restrained crops and no decorative filters.
- Segoe UI for readable editorial copy; Consolas for route times, map labels, and small field-guide notation.
- 16px cards, 10px controls, quiet borders, and shallow elevation.

## Interaction grammar

- Add/remove places with a clear toggle state.
- Move selected stops earlier or later with explicit controls.
- Search and filter without losing the current route.
- The map is a schematic orientation layer and explicitly says it is not turn-by-turn navigation.
- Clipboard export is a small utility action, surfaced with a transient status message.

## Responsive rules

On narrow screens, the route map leads the planner, the timeline follows, and the discovery grid becomes a single column. Navigation becomes a compact disclosure. All route editing remains keyboard accessible and visible.

## Named signature

**The line of the day:** every selected place becomes a numbered point on the schematic map and a corresponding moment in the timeline, making the plan feel like one connected day rather than a saved list.
